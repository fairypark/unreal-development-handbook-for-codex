# v0.2.13 — Risk-based Stage 2 elevation profiles

This release replaces the ambiguous Stage 2 instruction to create a side or
elevation profile "as appropriate" with an inspectable, risk-based decision
contract. It prevents agents from manufacturing every profile by default while
also preventing unsupported omission of vertical evidence.

## Included

- Defined the Area Composition Plan as one versioned evidence package rather
  than a required count of separate floor-plan, node-map, top-down, side, and
  elevation files. Review functions may share a sheet, occupy separate pages or
  layers, or link separately versioned artifacts when their identities and
  evidence remain inspectable.
- Added a Stage 2 `vertical_information_assessment` after the plan-view evidence
  is inspectable and before side/elevation production or package-scope
  finalization.
- Required the assessment to cover terrain relief, multi-level circulation,
  cliffs, overhangs, tunnels, bridges, sightline occlusion, and the vertical
  relationship between architecture and terrain.
- Added explicit `SUFFICIENT`, `INSUFFICIENT`, and `PENDING_EVIDENCE` outcomes.
  `INSUFFICIENT` requires named and scoped side/elevation profiles;
  `SUFFICIENT` requires an omission reason and risk-by-risk alternative
  evidence; `PENDING_EVIDENCE` blocks package finalization and Stage 2
  promotion.
- Required the agent to explain the result, evidence, proposed profile scope or
  omission, and remaining ambiguity to the user or designated approver before
  profile production.
- Repeated the contract in the world-design, validation, and AI-assisted
  development Skills and canonical chapters, including hard failures, evidence
  integrity, validation checklist, and common-mistake guidance.
- Added regression coverage that verifies the decision states, vertical-risk
  categories, user communication, package-versus-file distinction, Stage 2a
  placement lock, and independent Terrain Representation Review.

## Preserved gates and boundaries

- Stage 2 `PASS` still advances only to Stage 2a. It does not authorize the
  first content-bearing prototype placement.
- Stage 2a still requires its machine-readable source registry, zone-level
  quantitative contract, traceability, tolerances, and explicit placement
  decision.
- The vertical-information assessment does not select Landscape or Mesh
  Terrain and does not satisfy or bypass Stage 2t Terrain Representation
  Review.
- The Stage 2a schema and templates remain compatible. They continue to
  inventory whichever approved plan, section, elevation, or other sources
  actually control the prototype.
